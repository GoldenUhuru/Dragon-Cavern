# DC-P026 — Dragon Ethics

**Primary source:** D. J. Conway, *Mystical Dragon Magick*, p. 26 and discussion through pp. 27–30.
**Archived evidence:** *Williams_Dragon_Study_Record_2026-10-10.md*, earlier thematic visual study.
**Status:** LOCATED; ten-part code summarized but not transcribed verbatim; full-text verification and user approval pending.

## Page 26 — recorded themes
The study record identifies a **ten-part Dragon Ethics code** covering quiet action, inner truth, honesty, responsibility for balance, silence, respect for elders, readiness for difficulty, dignity, responsibility for one's character, and interconnected consequences. These are **themes from the archived summary**, not ten verified numbered quotations or necessarily a one-to-one mapping to the original ten clauses. The underlined closing passage stresses letting life and actions speak instead of trying to impress others.

The **Rule of Three** appears on p.26 as Conway's teaching concerning the return of deliberately directed magical power. Preserve attribution to the author.

## Pages 27–30 — related ethical discussion
- **p.27:** Hidden Dragon Heart, inner truth, dragon teachers, common sense, honesty, balance of the Multiverse, elders' knowledge, dignity and self-control. The Web of Life connects animate and inanimate things; actions have ripple effects whose full consequences are not always known.
- **p.27 aura claim:** Conway describes intentions/emotions as visible in auras; this is the author's claim, not verified scientific evidence.
- **p.28:** Distinguishes darkness from evil and discusses Light/Dark balance. Other reader's marginal objections concerning karma and Wiccan Rede are not William's views and must not be presented as printed text.
- **p.29:** Light, Dark and Chaos dragons are distinguished; Chaos dragons connected in Conway's model to Creative Void and dark-matter language.
- **p.30:** Use common sense and ethical judgment when interpreting messages; reject messages that conflict with ethics or morals.
