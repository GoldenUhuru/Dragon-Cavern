# DC-P054-P055 — Dragons of the Elements: Introduction

**Source:** D. J. Conway, *Mystical Dragon Magick*, pp. 54–55, heading at foot of p. 54.  
**Status:** Verified against supplied readable page photographs; awaiting William's approval.  
**Scope:** Opening paragraph and transition into p. 55; not a replacement for individual elemental leader entries.

## Opening (p. 54)
- The author states that European dragons differ **physically, mentally, and emotionally** from Eastern dragons.
- Students will work closely with **both kinds** during the Five Inner Rings.
- Their **colors, reactions, knowledge, and activities as teachers** may differ greatly.
- Nonetheless, the **dragon co-magician and other teachers** are said to help in every possible way.

## Transition (p. 55)
The author says elemental dragons **usually** match the colors attributed by many cultures to corresponding cardinal points:

| Direction | Element | Colors |
|---|---|---|
| East | Air | Yellow |
| South | Fire | Red |
| West | Water | Shades of blue |
| North | Earth | Soil colors through dark green of ancient forests |

**Qualifier:** 'usually' must not be turned into an absolute rule about every individual dragon.

### Four Hidden Dragons of Wisdom (p. 55)
The author says Asian dragon studies alone mention these four great dragons. They exist **in the center of the Five Rings**, protect the **four Hidden Gates** to higher Mystery School areas, and guard those gates. Keep them distinct from the four directional elemental-dragon leaders and from the general Eastern-versus-European introductory distinction.

## Atlas / visual-design implications
- Do not homogenize European and Eastern dragons into one body type; **the passage itself gives no detailed physical anatomy**, so specific visual differences need other page evidence.
- Do not assign a sex to an elemental leader unless source text does.
- Directional color scheme is described as usual correspondence, not categorical identity.
- The Four Hidden Dragons of Wisdom have a center-of-Five-Rings guarding relationship; do not invent chamber geometry or a route without other source evidence.
